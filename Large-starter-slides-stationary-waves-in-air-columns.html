12.6 Stationary waves in air columns

A string of length 0.80 m is fixed at both ends and vibrates in its fundamental mode.

a) Sketch the standing wave pattern.

b) State the wavelength of the wave in terms of the string length.

a) nodes at both ends; one antinode in the middle

b) λ = 2L

A stretched string has length 1.20 m. The speed of transverse wave on the string is 60 ms-1.

a) Calculate the frequency of the fundamental.

b) Calculate the frequency of the third harmonic.

a) fundamental frequency = 25 Hz

b) 3rd harmonic 75 Hz

A string fixed at both ends is vibrating in its 5th harmonic.

How many antinodes are formed along the string?

b) Explain why the wave speed is the same for all harmonics, even the frequencies are different.

a) 5 antinodes

b) Wave speed is the same for all harmonics because it depends only on the tension in the string and the mass per unit length. These do not change when the harmonic changes.

A student increases the tension in a string while keeping its length and mass per unit length constant.

a) State how the wave speed changes and explain why.

b) Describe what happens to the frequencies of all harmonics.

a) Wave speed increases. Increasing tension increases wave speed.

b) The frequencies of all harmonics increases. Length stays constant->wavelength is the same. Speed increases->frequency increases v=fλ
