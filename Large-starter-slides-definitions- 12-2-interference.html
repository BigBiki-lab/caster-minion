12.2 Interference

COHERENT WAVES

COHERENT WAVES

Two wave sources, or waves, that are coherent have a constant phase difference. In order to be coherent the two waves must have the same frequency.

PHASE DIFFERENCE

PHASE DIFFERENCE

The difference between the displacements of particles along a wave, or the difference between the displacements of particles on different waves.

CONSTRUCTIVE INTERFERENCE

CONSTRUCTIVE INTERFERENCE

Superposition of two waves in phase (phase difference = 2nℼ; path difference = nλ) so that the resultant wave has greater amplitude than the original waves.

DESTRUCTIVE INTERFERENCE

DESTRUCTIVE INTERFERENCE

Superposition of two waves in antiphase (phase difference = (2n+1)ℼ; path difference = (n+1/2)λ) so that the waves cancel each other out and the resultant wave has smaller amplitude than the original waves.
